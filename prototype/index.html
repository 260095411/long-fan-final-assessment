The Yes/No Contract

An age gate advertises a single bit: over 18, or not. This prototype follows that bit backwards, to the moment a face or a document leaves the browser, and forwards again, to the moment a person is refused with no way to answer back.

Simulated data only. No photographs, documents or personal details are collected or transmitted. Every uncertain step is labelled as uncertain.

Research summary

What the reading established, in four moves

Age assurance is now a duty on services likely to be accessed by children under the Online Safety Act, and Ofcom's guidance asks providers to judge a method on four criteria — technical accuracy, robustness, reliability and fairness — plus two principles, accessibility and interoperability.

Knight-Georgetown Institute (2026) tested the methods actually on the market and concluded that no single signal satisfies all four of its own criteria — baseline accuracy, circumvention resistance, availability and privacy. Accuracy is bought with availability, or with privacy, and never all at once.

ICO guidance adds a narrower obligation: consent must be freely given, specific and informed, and the Age Appropriate Design Code asks for a design decision made in the child's interest, not a legal tick.

The question this prototype pursues follows from that gap: if the answer arrives as a single bit, what was captured, what was kept, and what a person can do when the bit is wrong.

02

The issue this prototype investigates

Not whether age checks are good or bad. One specific thing: the binary answer conceals both the record and the error.

Claim. An age gate presents a threshold — a single bit — as if it were the whole exchange. But the bit is produced by a third-party evaluator that received an image or a document, is stored by the service against an account, and is returned as a decision the person cannot inspect or contest. The threshold format is a privacy claim and a dispute-suppression device at the same time.

      Why a working website does not prove the claim

      A prototype shows that a flow can be arranged this way. It cannot show that one arrangement is safer, fairer or more consentful. Those claims rest on the evidence below, and on what the evidence does not establish. The purpose here is to make the exchange visible, not to recommend View B.
